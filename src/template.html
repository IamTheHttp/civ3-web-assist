Civ3 Empire Viewer

Open a save to see your cities

How it works

Get started

  1. Load rules (conquests.biq)… once. It's in your Conquests folder; the page remembers it.
  2. Open save…, or drop a .SAV file on the page.
  3. Optional: Watch autosave folder… reloads the newest save each turn (Edge or Chrome).

Your files stay on your computer. Settings and history stay in this browser.

Buttons

Open save…
Read a save file.
Load rules…
Read conquests.biq: names, corruption model, what-ifs, tiers. A save with custom rules (a mod or scenario) uses its own rules instead; Help lists them under In these rules.
Watch autosave folder…
Pick Saves\Auto; the newest save loads by itself.
View
Switch between sets of columns.
Columns…
Choose this view's columns. Clear all columns starts from empty; New view makes your own.
Player
Shown only for a hot-seat game (several human players): pick your civilization. The choice is kept per game, and History, Milestones and what-ifs are kept per player.
Side menu
Summary, Milestones, History, Diplomacy, Governments, Tiers, Settings and Diagnostics open in a panel on the right; the table stays where it is. Drag its left edge to resize it (double-click: default width), or ⤢ Expand to fill the window; each panel remembers its own. Esc returns an expanded panel to the side, then shuts it.
Tiers
Name corruption tiers and set which buildings each tier should have, from which city size.
Search (above the table)
Matches any text in the table: library, a city name, max, no temple. Several words must all match. Press / to jump there, Esc to clear.
Problems only
Cities in disorder or close to it, starving, or with resisters.
Needs attention
Cities missing a building their tier expects.
Clear filters
Shows when a column is filtered; removes every filter in this view.
Export CSV
The table as a spreadsheet file.
Try government
Your cities under another government: the tiles' food, shields and commerce (a government with a trade bonus, such as Republic, adds +1 commerce on tiles with commerce; one with a tile penalty, such as Despotism, takes 1 off a yield above 2), corruption and waste, building upkeep and unit support. War weariness and happiness aren't included. The Governments panel compares them all.
Try slider
Off: gold, science, luxury and the building gains use the slider from your save. On: they use the slider you set, for example 50% science and 50% gold, so a Marketplace isn't judged by a low gold rate. Happy and unhappy citizens aren't recalculated.
Reset what-ifs
Undo every tick you changed, and go back to your government and slider.
◐ Auto
Theme: Auto (your system), Light or Dark.

The table

Sort
Click a column name. Click again to reverse.
▾ Filter
On each column, as in a spreadsheet: sort, a condition (greater than, between, contains…) or tick the values to keep. A blue ▾ marks a filtered column. Filters and sorting use the real numbers, so a what-if tick never hides or moves a city; each view keeps its own filters.
Building columns
One column per building of your game's rules that reduces corruption or adds science, gold, luxury or shields, with a short name such as CH (Courthouse) or Lib (Library). Ticked: the city has it. Tick a box to try a building (a city counts only its best power plant). In Columns…, a group (Science, Production…) takes every such building. Changed numbers show now → then; an amber outline marks a tick that isn't real. Buildings made obsolete by a tech you know aren't shown.
The gain next to a tick
What one building would gain the city per turn, after upkeep. The colour is the gain in gold a turn, by the worth of a shield, food, gold, science and luxury set in Settings (default a shield or food = 4 gold): green above 10, amber 5–10, grey 0–5, red a loss. Point at a cell for the cost and the turns to pay it back. The columns sort largest gain first. A building that needs a tech you don't know shows the tech instead (Settings can show it anyway). Set the limits, the worths and icons or words in Settings.
Tier
Pick a tier to fix it for that city; (auto) follows corruption.
Attention
Amber: missing. Green: being built now.
Colours
Red: serious (half or more lost, disorder). Amber: watch it. ~: the corruption model doesn't match the game in that city.
Hover
Point at a column name or a number for more detail.

Panels

Summary cards
Your whole empire. Point at a line to see how it's worked out. With what-ifs (ticks, Try slider, Try government), changed figures show now → then.
Milestones
Targets to reach by a turn, per game: ✓ met, in progress, or ✗ missed. Edit, add or delete them; the defaults are only starting points. In History, ⚑ marks a deadline and ◆ a target.
History
Each save you open stores its turn. Pick a chart and a city; the dashed line is the turn you have loaded. Add saves… adds older saves at once.
Governments
Your empire's totals under each government, with your slider (or the tried one). Click a row to try it in the table.
Diplomacy
Civilizations you've met: their attitude toward you (point at it for the reasons, such as your war declarations on them), government, cities, culture against yours, war, embassy, right of passage, your allies against them, alliances against you, the techs and luxuries each side can trade, their treasury and the most gold per turn they pay in a deal. Below: your war weariness points with each enemy and what they do to a city of size 12.
Diagnostics
What the page read from your files. Download debug report if something looks wrong.
